Three parties, one rule

Money moves only when the merchant's live terms still match what you approved. The merchant checks; your agent can't talk its way past it.

1

Your agent shops

It searches the shop and asks for an offer. Every request it makes is signed with its own key, so the merchant knows exactly which agent is calling.

2

You approve exact terms

The item, the price in your currency, the exchange rate and how long it's locked, the delivery. You also set the most you'll pay.

3

The merchant countersigns

At checkout the merchant re-prices everything live. Same terms: it charges and signs a receipt. Anything moved: nothing is charged, and your agent asks you again.

How Countersign works

The idea

AI agents can now buy things for you. The risk is that the price, the exchange rate or the item changes between the moment you said yes and the moment money moves, and nobody checks.

Countersign makes the merchant check. Your approval is a precise statement of what you agreed to. At checkout the merchant re-prices everything live and only charges if it still matches, then signs a receipt you can verify yourself.

Try it in four clicks

  • 1. Send your agent. Type what you want, or pick one of the examples.
  • 2. Read the terms. The middle panel shows the price in your currency, the rate and its countdown, and the delivery.
  • 3. Change the world (optional). Press a button in the merchant's panel before you approve.
  • 4. Approve. The merchant either countersigns and charges, or stops and your agent asks you again.

The merchant's buttons

  • Nudge the rate +1%charges
    Your ceiling has a little room above the price, so a small move is fine.
  • Move the rate +5%asks again

  • Raise price 12%asks again
    The merchant's own price no longer matches the one you approved.
  • Delay deliveryasks again
    You approved 5–7 days. Twelve to fifteen is a different deal.
  • Send botsrefused
    Unsigned, signed by a stranger, signed for browsing instead of paying, and an approval swapped in transit. The edge refuses all four.

Words on this page

Rate lock
The exchange rate the merchant guarantees for a while (here, an hour). The countdown shows how long is left.
The most you'll pay (ceiling)
Your limit in your currency. It starts a little above the price so small market moves don't interrupt you.
Countersign
The merchant's second signature on your deal: it re-checks your approval at the moment of payment.
Agent signature (TAP)
Every request your agent makes is signed with its key, using Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol, so the merchant knows who is asking and whether it may pay.
Receipt
What was charged, against which approval, signed with the merchant's key. Press Verify to check it, or Tamper, then verify to see an edited one fail.

What's real here

Every agent request goes through the real merchant edge with real signatures, and the receipts are real Ed25519 signatures you can check in your browser. The shop, its stock and the exchange rate are simulated for this demo, and each visit gets its own private copy.

The same merchant also runs behind Airwallex's agentic commerce MCP, where Claude shops and Airwallex takes a test-card payment. See the run book in the repository.
